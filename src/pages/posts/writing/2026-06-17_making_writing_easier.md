It feels easy to start writing when I'm in a cafe or in a waiting room, but it's not that easy when I'm at home.
Sometimes, just opening a notebook and holding a pen feels like too much.

Am I tired of writing? Have I lost interest in writing? Can writing be tiresome?  
Whatever the reason, the important thing for me right now is to be able to organize my thoughts. And I believe writing is necessary for that.

Here are some tips I think would help me write.

## Preparation
- Take notes.  
Take notes when you read, when you think, when you reflect on your day (whenever you're not writing). 
Trying to write from scratch is hard, but if you have a habit of taking notes, you can collect the ones that are related to the topic in one place before writing so you know what to write. Separate gathering information from writing.
- Make an outline.  
Don't start writing drafts right away after you collect notes. Put them in order so it will be easier to write drafts. Think about how the structure should be before actually writing.

## Writing drafts
- Don't try to write in one go. Write a crappy draft first and stop trying to make it perfect from the start.  
You can delete and add paragraphs later in the rewriting phase.
- Write like you're speaking to someone, like you're playing, or like you're having fun.  
When your imagination starts to take you in an unexpected direction, just let it. You can edit it later. Don't stop the flow.
- Don't force yourself to write. If you get lost and have no idea what to write next, stop there.

## Tools (when you don't use keyboards)
- Use a pen that feels comfortable to write with (you can use a pencil as well, of course).
- Use a notebook (or just a piece of paper!) that you don't feel guilty about when it gets messy.   
I prefer cheap pocket notebooks to neat, pretty A5-size notebooks.
I think allowing yourself to write messy stuff makes it easier to write.

## Rewriting
In this phase, you start editing and rewriting.  
Delete the unnecessary sentences and add the missing explanations. Repeat this process until you feel "Hmm, this isn't so bad after all!"

I won't force myself to follow these tips though. Sometimes writing in one go can be better, I believe.
