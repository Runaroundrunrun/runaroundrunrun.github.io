Reading these posts made me realize that I've been spending more time in front of screens than actually enjoying time without the Internet.
- Dave Rupert, <a class="icon-new-tab" target="_blank" href="https://daverupert.com/2026/09/i-wish-you-the-best-in-the-offline/">I wish you the best in the Offline</a> 
- Imperfect, <a class="icon-new-tab" target="_blank" href="https://imperfect.bearblog.dev/online-and-offline/">online and offline</a>
- Salma Alam-Naylor, <a class="icon-new-tab" target="_blank" href="https://whitep4nth3r.com/blog/goodbye-forever-probably/">Goodbye, forever, probably.</a> 

Now the world behind the screens occupies big part of my life, not "real" things and "real" human beings.

I see my offline friends less than before, I watch contents on platforms like Netflix more than going to a movie theater, I read downloaded blog posts more than printed books, and I become obsessed with friendships or relationships on screens.  

I don't want to think being online is completely a bad thing, but life shouldn't be lived this way, right? 

When I first set up this website, [I wanted to learn about myself and the world through writing and I wanted to keep the stuff I create](/posts/web-dev/2026-05-08_on_setting_up_this_website/), but these days it feels like I wrote posts to keep the streaks and drew to share.

Posts, notes, and all the stuff I create should be the products of my life, not the other way around.  
I just seemed to forget that lately.

I can't decide if I should go offline yet, and I'm not sure if I can.  
But I should keep in mind that there's always an option to go offline...
